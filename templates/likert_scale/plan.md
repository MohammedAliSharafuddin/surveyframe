## Study

**Purpose:** A thesis questionnaire measuring how well organised
attendees found an event, on a 4-item Likert scale with 1 reverse-worded
item.

**Research problem:** A student’s thesis needs a short scale of
perceived event organisation, and the scale must hold together before
its score is used.

| Objective | Research questions |
|----|----|
| O1: Describe how organised the event felt to attendees. | RQ1 (How does the scale score overall?) |
| O2: Check that the organisation scale is reliable. | RQ2 (Is the scale internally consistent?); RQ3 (Is the scale internally consistent according to omega?); RQ4 (Does any item sit poorly with the rest?) |

## The questionnaire

The questions in the order respondents see them.

1.  The event ran to time. (Rating scale). Options: Strongly disagree,
    Disagree, Neither agree nor disagree, Agree, Strongly agree.
2.  It was easy to find my way around. (Rating scale). Options: Strongly
    disagree, Disagree, Neither agree nor disagree, Agree, Strongly
    agree.
3.  Staff were helpful when I had a question. (Rating scale). Options:
    Strongly disagree, Disagree, Neither agree nor disagree, Agree,
    Strongly agree.
4.  I struggled to work out where to go. (Rating scale). Options:
    Strongly disagree, Disagree, Neither agree nor disagree, Agree,
    Strongly agree.

## Pre-declared analysis plan

### RQ1: How does the scale score overall?

**Method:** Scale score descriptive statistics

### RQ2: Is the scale internally consistent?

**Planned decision rule:** Treat the scale as reliable when Cronbach’s
alpha is .70 or above.

**Method:** Cronbach’s alpha (internal consistency)

**Method reference:** (Cronbach, 1951)

### RQ3: Is the scale internally consistent according to omega?

**Planned decision rule:** Treat the scale as reliable when omega is .70
or above.

**Method:** McDonald’s omega (internal consistency)

**Method reference:** (Cronbach, 1951) (McDonald, 1999)

### RQ4: Does any item sit poorly with the rest?

**Planned decision rule:** Review any item whose corrected item-total
correlation is below .30.

**Method:** Item diagnostics
